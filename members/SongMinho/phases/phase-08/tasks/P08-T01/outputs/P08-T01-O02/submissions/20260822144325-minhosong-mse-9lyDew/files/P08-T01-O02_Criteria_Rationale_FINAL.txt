P08-T01-O02 — DEVICE-LEVEL PASS / MARGINAL / FAIL BASIS
Decision status: APPROVED BY EXPERIMENT LEAD BEFORE P8 ELECTRICAL SWEEP
Approval time recorded in this local package: 2026-08-21 13:37 KST
Scope: P8 Device-level classification only. This is NOT a DRAM-pass guarantee.

1. Why P2 matched Single is the outer boundary
P5 is explicitly framed as improvement of the L-H-L Multi-Metal structure against the matched Single-Metal baseline under common extraction conditions.
Therefore P2 is used as the point where the optimized structure has lost its claimed advantage:
- Ion lower than P2 -> drive improvement has disappeared.
- Ioff higher than P2 -> off-leakage improvement has disappeared.
- DIBL higher than P2 -> electrostatic improvement has disappeared.
This is a comparative research boundary, not an industry specification.

2. Why P5 Nominal 35/67 is the 100% optimized anchor
P5 selected 35/67 nm as the balanced 2D Nominal before P8.
It is therefore a natural pre-P8 optimized reference point.
Using it does NOT mean 35/67 is a universal physical ideal; it is the project's optimized anchor.

3. Why the 50% retained-improvement line is used for Ion/Ioff/DIBL
P2 is defined as 0% retained optimized benefit and P5 Nominal as 100%.
The 50% line is the transparent midpoint between two pre-existing anchors.
It is intentionally an operational research rule, not a claimed universal device-physics threshold.

P5 49-point Forward back-test was performed before this approval:
- 50% rule over all 49: PASS 9 / MARGINAL 6 / FAIL 34.
- The FAIL count is fixed by the P2 outer boundary and stayed 34 for 25–75% tier thresholds.
- Among the 9 P5-known points inside the planned P8 domain: PASS 8 / MARGINAL 1 / FAIL 0 at 50%.
- That local classification is unchanged for 25%, 33%, 40%, and 50%, so 50% is not located on an unstable local classification cliff.
Key anchors behaved coherently:
  35/67 PASS
  33/67 PASS
  35/65 PASS
  37/63 MARGINAL
  39/61 FAIL due to DIBL worse than P2
  41/59 and more aggressive short-M2 cases FAIL with documented leakage/SCE degradation.

4. Why Ioff is evaluated in log space
Ioff is a positive leakage current whose research relevance is multiplicative/order-level.
P5 explicitly uses strong/order-level Ioff increase as rejection evidence.
Using log10 space makes the retained-improvement fraction consistent with multiplicative leakage behavior rather than treating equal absolute ampere changes as equally meaningful.

5. Why DIBL and Ion remain linear
Ion and DIBL were interpreted as ordinary performance/electrostatic metrics in the P5 comparison, without evidence requiring log scaling.
Linear interpolation between the matched-Single and optimized-Nominal anchors is therefore the simpler, more transparent choice.

6. Why GIDL is full-grid but NOT a 50% tier metric
P5 used GIDL as a shortlist/guard rather than as an all-49 ranking objective.
The measured shortlist GIDL values were all close to the P5 Nominal and far below P2, so there is no pre-P8 evidence that a '50% retained GIDL improvement' is a meaningful PASS/MARGINAL separator.
P8 therefore:
- calculates GIDL for every ACTIVE geometry,
- fails a geometry if GIDL > P2 matched Single,
- reports GIDL/RIF,
- but does not use GIDL=50% to split PASS from MARGINAL.
This preserves P5 research logic while producing a complete P8 leakage guard map.

7. Overall classification
FAIL:
- numerical run/extraction invalid, OR
- Ion < P2, OR
- Ioff > P2, OR
- DIBL > P2, OR
- GIDL > P2.

PASS:
- all required numerical results valid,
- Ion/Ioff/DIBL each retain >=50% of P5 improvement over P2,
- GIDL <= P2.

MARGINAL:
- no core metric is worse than P2,
- GIDL guard passes,
- but one or more of Ion/Ioff/DIBL retain <50% of P5 improvement.

8. Numeric thresholds
Ion PASS threshold  = 9.475605928656140e-06 A
Ioff PASS threshold = 1.687325120161349e-15 A
DIBL PASS threshold = 2.766079953989873 mV/V
GIDL hard guard     = <= 2.879053060674510e-14 A

9. Final-study interpretation
The P8 class is a Device-level prediction.
P9 independently tests whether the P8 Device-level prediction agrees with actual 1T1C DRAM behavior and explicitly analyzes False-Pass / False-Fail.
